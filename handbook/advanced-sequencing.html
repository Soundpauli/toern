Advanced Sequencing

Mutes, note-shift, copy & paste, and random / auto-fill. Page-row layout and hint colours live in Pages+More; core draw modes in Sequencing.

Performance fills

While playing, select a voice and press touch 4 + 5 together (external panel: C + D). FILL flashes in that voice’s color for one second. The fill starts on the next page’s first beat, builds for two pages, and finishes with one velocity-127 hit on the following downbeat.

Release both inputs, select another voice, and repeat before the page ends to queue several voices together. They keep independent timing; re-requesting one voice restarts that voice next page. The normal mute, fader and V gain controls still apply, so full note velocity does not override a muted or silent voice. Requested fills leave grid notes unchanged; grid F/F conditions use the same buildup and final-hit timing.

Where these tools live

These gestures need the cursor on the page row (row 16, y==16) — usually in single mode — or a voice row for mutes. If the cursor is not on row 16, page-row edits do not run. Hint colours on row 1 tell you which encoder is armed — see Pages+More for the colour key.

Mutes

From a voice row (not the page row), long-press encoder 2 opens a transient mute / solo overlay: other lanes mute so the current voice solos; release to restore. Short-press encoder 2 on a voice row toggles that lane’s mute and the flag sticks.

Mute overlay: muted lanes dim, active lanes bright.

While you hold encoder 2, the other knobs stay useful:

  • Encoder 3 (green) — fast filter for the current voice, same as from the grid
  • Encoder 4 (pink) — scroll a random sample playlist (?> / ?<); short press loads the last preview onto that voice
  • Encoder 1 — reverse the current sample (channels 1–8)

On the page row, encoder 2 short is note-shift (single mode) — not mute. Step onto a voice row first if you want the overlay.

R-mode’s temporary full mute (encoder 1 on a voice row) is a different path — see Sequencing → R mode and SETT → MUTE for the custom unmute set.

Note-shift

Note-shift moves real step data in time and across rows — it edits the pattern. There is no separate global “transpose” page: sliding pitch/row here is the in-grid transpose. To change pitch without rewriting steps, use detune and octave in Sound design instead.

Enter from single mode + page row with a short press on the white encoder 2. The firmware snapshots every other voice, then lets you slide only the focused voice. Hints switch to bright green (enc 1) and bright blue (enc 4). A grey marquee crawls on row 1 while you work.

SETT → SWAP — exchange voice notes

Choose two voices with encoder 2 and encoder 3, then click encoder 4 to apply. Encoder 1 exits. Supported voices are 1–8, 11, 13 and 14. The exchange covers the complete 256-step pattern, including notes on pages outside the current view.

Hold encoder 2 or 3 while turning it to transpose the notes leaving that side by −8 to +8 octaves; each side starts at 0. The signed octave chooser appears only while the encoder is held. Otherwise the display shows the two voice numbers. Stored absolute MIDI pitches are clamped to 0–127.

Step velocity, probability and conditions follow the notes. Sound settings and sample assignments stay on their original voices. Choosing the same voice twice changes nothing. SWAP reuses the copy/paste scratch buffer; arm a fresh copy afterward if you need to paste.

Copy & paste

On the page row, encoder 4 short is copy / paste. Draw copies the whole edit page (all voices); single copies only the focused voice (you can paste onto a different voice after changing focus).

Copy armed

After the first encoder 4 tap, encoder 1 stays blue (cancel) and encoder 4 turns green (paste). c+p sits in yellow in the centre. Navigate to the destination page, then tap encoder 4 again to paste. Encoder 1 short cancels without pasting. The same copy / paste also runs from FN + touch 2.

Companion & auto-fill

Where in-grid randomize works: cursor on the page row, hold FN (touch 5), then long-press encoder 2 (0200). Single mode rewrites the focused voice. Draw mode writes a multi-voice page. Encoder 1 long on that row deletes the page instead.

That long press regenerates only the focused voice on the current edit page. The companion reads the other voices across the active pattern, weighting this page and its neighbours most strongly. It follows their accents, velocities and tonal centre while using the focused voice’s role:

  • 1 kick, 2 snare, 3 closed hi-hat, 4 clap, 5 tom
  • 6 sample bass, 7 keys, 8 vocals / pads
  • 11 synth bass, 13 and 14 synth keys

Rhythm uses a hybrid: kick/snare retain musical anchors, but snare backbeats can move one step early or late and react to kick, clap and ensemble density. Sparse Euclidean candidates give hats, claps and toms varied pulse counts and full 16-step rotations; claps may reinforce a snare or carry an empty backbeat. On a 32-step page, the second half receives a related rotation instead of copying the first half. Every role can land on different pitch rows. Bass reacts to kick, existing bass and harmonic activity while favouring root and fifth. Bass, keys and pads share a page-level key, scale and four-slot chord progression (e.g. minor i–VI–III–VII); sample keys and pads stay monophonic; synth lanes 13+14 form a two-note chord with one tone on each lane. Channels 1–8 never receive stacked chords. Channel 11 is capped at three voices, though its bass role normally generates a single-note line. Note picks weight ~60% current chord tones, ~30% other scale tones and ~10% chromatic passing tones. Tempo still thins embellishments. If every other voice is empty, the companion invents a varied 4/4 starting point rather than replaying one fixed template. Repeat the long press for a fresh interpretation.

Clear and randomize have no undo. If you wipe the wrong page, recover from whatever you last saved under PATT.

For multi-page generative fill, use MODE → AUTO. AUTO uses the same companion engine, learns from its orange base-page range, and writes coordinated variations into the following green pages. An empty base now creates a complete starter ensemble.
