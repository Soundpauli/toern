The BPM screen

Open BPM from the menu to set tempo, matrix brightness, and the internal vs external clock source. Audio output level is separate — see VOL → MAIN.

Range: 40–300 BPM, with one decimal place on a single row. The decimal point is white; the tenths digit, encoder 1 and its indicator are violet in every clock mode. In INT, encoder 4 edits whole BPM and encoder 1 edits tenths (.0–.9), for example 120.3 BPM. Tap tempo: repeated clean taps on the BPM screen set the internal tempo.

In MIDI slave mode, incoming clock continues to update the measured BPM while playing. The display shows one decimal place; that display precision does not limit the internal clock measurement.

Variant A — Internal clock (master)

Cyan whole BPM, a white decimal point and violet tenths on one row. The green right-pointing arrow shows internal master clock; encoder 3 is green and encoder 1 is violet.

Variant B — MIDI external clock, locked

Green tempo readout with one decimal place, red left-pointing arrow on the right showing TRS MIDI clock is being received. The arrow blinks with the clock. Encoder 3 click switches to analog EXT (variant D). Encoder 4 offsets the stored counter without overriding the live clock.

Variant C — MIDI external clock, unstable

Red tempo readout with one decimal place and a red left-pointing arrow — the measurement is still settling or no MIDI clock is arriving. The first tempo estimate needs six clock intervals; a steady source typically settles within a quarter note. If it stays red, check the cable and the master.

Variant D — Analog EXT (CLK-IN)

Orange whole BPM shows measured analog tempo; the decimal point stays white and the tenths digit violet. The orange left-pointing arrow flashes on each CLK-IN pulse. Pulses drive the sequencer immediately. Set input PPQN in MIDI → PQ-I; encoder 3 click returns to MIDI EXT.
